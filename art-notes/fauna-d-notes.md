The atlas contains 18 animals, IDs 54–71, arranged in three columns and six rows. It uses natural-history rendering with individually scaled animals for arcade gameplay. It is a species-inspired illustration set, not a scientific measurement sheet. ## Extraction and landmarks

The PNG is 1024 × 1536. There are exactly 18 major four-connected alpha components (>500 pixels at alpha >8), one per animal. Tiny detached antialiasing specks are intentionally excluded by the existing flood-fill extractor. Component IDs in source order are 1, 2, 3, 62, 7, 8, 102, 105, 101, 126, 125, 124, 141, 139, 140, 157, 158, 159. The irregular cell boundaries are represented by precise component bounds in `dist/assets/fauna-bounds.json`; do not split the image into equal rectangular cells.

`mouth` gives normalized x/y coordinates relative to the trimmed component **with the extractor's 2-pixel transparent border on all four sides**. `body` gives normalized center x/y and ellipse radii x/y in that same coordinate system. Body ellipses omit long tails, fins, claws and tentacle tips. Shark mouth anchors sit below the head, with the goblin shark's long nose explicitly excluded. Nautilus and cephalopod mouth anchors are at the arm roots rather than the tentacle ends. Sea-pig and isopod anchors approximate the feeding region on the underside of the forward head. The art does not anatomically reveal those underside mouthparts.
